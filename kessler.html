Owen Kessler, MD

Medical fiction grounded in clinical reality.

Owen Kessler writes medical fiction grounded in clinical detail and the pressure of decisions made in real time. He is the author of the Triage Quartet.

The Triage Quartet

Triage

Triage Quartet, Book One

Nobody was coming. That was never the bad news.

Dr. Owen Kessler spent twenty years getting ready for the end of the world — the well, the diesel, the fourteen-month pantry, the range cut into ten acres of east Bexar County. Then the grid over San Antonio dies, and stays dead, and the disaster that arrives is not the one he prepared for. The prepared man discovers, thirty-four days deep and one patient at a time, that the barn was never the preparation. The neighbors were.

Golden Hour

Triage Quartet, Book Two

The lights came back. The wrong things came back with them.

Eighty-one days after the grid died, power returns to Salado Creek — and Dr. Owen Kessler, the man who held a Texas county together in the dark, learns that the hardest hour is the one after the rescue. Out past the reconnected feeders, a disciplined crew is reading his county’s hand-built network the way Owen reads a patient — hunting for the soft place, planning to take it.

Edema

Triage Quartet, Book Three

Sixty-one came out of the water. The arithmetic was never the point.

Three years after the grid came back, a stalled system parks over the watershed on a Friday evening — during a retirement party — and the Salado starts climbing a tenth of a foot at a time. A flood novel told hour by hour in gauge readings and radio logs, in which Owen Kessler spends most of the book being the patient, and the county he built comes for him with love and paperwork.
